# The Integrity Metrics Framework

**Making the Invisible Visible: Quantifying Risk Avoidance, Trust Velocity, and Values Alignment for Small-to-Medium Businesses**

*Companion Resource: The Return on Integrity*

## Why This Framework Exists

Risk avoidance is invisible. The lawsuit you never faced, the customer who stayed, the reputation crisis that didn't happen—these outcomes have no natural advocate in quarterly reviews. This framework provides SMBs with measurable indicators that make integrity visible, fundable, and defensible.

## 1. Intervention Metrics

*Measuring the Friction That Works*

These metrics capture moments when your "moral friction" actively prevented drift. They answer the question: "Are our checkpoints catching anything?" High intervention rates indicate a healthy system where humans are exercising judgment—not a broken process.

### 1.1 Veto Rate

**Definition**  
The percentage of AI-generated decisions or recommendations that were reviewed and subsequently overridden, modified, or rejected by a human decision-maker within a defined period.

**What It Measures**  
Direct evidence that your human-in-the-loop checkpoints are functioning. A non-zero veto rate confirms that humans are actively exercising judgment rather than rubber-stamping AI outputs. Measures the effectiveness of your "Integrity Veto" governance mechanism.

**Calculation**  
```
Veto Rate = (AI Decisions Overridden ÷ Total AI Decisions Reviewed) × 100

Example: 15 overrides ÷ 200 reviewed decisions × 100 = 7.5% Veto Rate
```

---

### 1.2 Escalation Frequency

**Definition**  
The count and rate of automated processes that triggered a human review checkpoint, either through confidence thresholds, rule-based triggers, or anomaly detection within your AI systems.

**What It Measures**  
Shows that your system is actively asking "Is this acceptable?" before proceeding. High escalation frequency indicates your AI recognizes its limitations. This metric reflects the "Judging" phase of the Analog OS—the system's built-in uncertainty about edge cases.

**Calculation**  
```
Escalation Frequency = Total Escalations ÷ Time Period

Escalation Rate = (Escalated Decisions ÷ Total Automated Decisions) × 100

Example: 45 escalations in 30 days = 1.5 escalations/day
45 escalations ÷ 3,000 total decisions × 100 = 1.5% Escalation Rate
```

---

### 1.3 Pause-to-Proceed Ratio

**Definition**  
The ratio of decisions that required human deliberation (pause) before proceeding versus decisions that were automatically approved without human intervention. A higher ratio indicates a more deliberate, values-conscious system.

**What It Measures**  
The degree of intentionality in your automated processes. This metric distinguishes between "automation by design" and "automation by default." It answers: "How much of our operation runs on autopilot versus conscious choice?"

**Calculation**  
```
Pause-to-Proceed Ratio = Decisions Requiring Human Review : Auto-Approved Decisions

Example: 150 reviewed : 850 auto-approved = 1:5.67 ratio

Alternative percentage: (150 ÷ 1,000) × 100 = 15% Deliberation Rate
```

---

### 1.4 Close Call Log Count

**Definition**  
The total number of documented incidents where a potential values violation, compliance issue, or integrity breach was identified and prevented before it reached customers, the public, or caused measurable harm.

**What It Measures**  
Makes "what almost happened" concrete and countable. This is the most direct measure of risk avoidance—each logged close call represents a potential crisis that was averted. Creates organizational memory of near-misses that would otherwise be forgotten.

**Calculation**  
```
Close Call Log Count = Sum of Documented Near-Miss Incidents per Period

Close Call Trend = (Current Period Count - Previous Period Count) ÷ Previous Period Count × 100

Example: 12 close calls in Q3, 8 in Q2 = (12-8) ÷ 8 × 100 = 50% increase

Note: Increase may indicate better detection, not worse performance
```

> **SMB Implementation:** Create a simple shared spreadsheet or dedicated Slack channel where team members log entries in the format: "I stopped [X] from happening because [Y]." Review weekly. The act of documentation itself reinforces the value of vigilance.

## 2. Trust Velocity Metrics

*Compounding vs. Spreading*

"Trust compounds. Distrust spreads." These metrics track whether your integrity investments are generating compounding returns in relationship capital or whether erosion is occurring beneath the surface of your revenue numbers.

### 2.1 Relationship Longevity Index

**Definition**  
The weighted average duration of active customer relationships, tracked as a trend over rolling periods. Measures how long customers stay with your business and whether that duration is increasing, stable, or declining.

**What It Measures**  
Long-term trust accumulation. If trust is compounding, customers stay longer even as your business grows. A declining index—especially during growth—signals that new acquisition is masking relationship erosion. This is a leading indicator of future revenue stability.

**Calculation**  
```
RLI = Σ(Customer Tenure in Months) ÷ Total Active Customers

RLI Velocity = (Current Period RLI - Prior Period RLI) ÷ Prior Period RLI × 100

Example: Average tenure = 18 months (current), 16 months (prior year)
RLI Velocity = (18-16) ÷ 16 × 100 = +12.5% (trust compounding)
```

---

### 2.2 Forgiveness Rate

**Definition**  
The percentage of customers who experience a documented service failure, complaint, or negative interaction and choose to remain as active customers within a defined follow-up period (typically 90-180 days).

**What It Measures**  
The depth of accumulated trust. High forgiveness rates indicate that customers have built enough positive relationship capital to weather problems. This metric reveals whether your integrity investments have created genuine resilience or if relationships are transactional and fragile.

**Calculation**  
```
Forgiveness Rate = (Customers Retained Post-Failure ÷ Customers Who Experienced Failure) × 100

Example: 85 retained ÷ 100 who experienced failure × 100 = 85% Forgiveness Rate

Track by failure type for deeper insight (billing errors vs. service quality vs. communication lapses)
```

---

### 2.3 Referral-from-Tenure

**Definition**  
The number and percentage of new customer referrals that originate from customers with tenure exceeding a defined threshold (typically 2+ years). Isolates referral activity from your most established, trust-tested relationships.

**What It Measures**  
Mature trust expression. New customers may refer due to initial enthusiasm; long-tenured customers refer based on sustained experience. High referral rates from 2+ year customers indicate that your integrity holds up over time—the ultimate validation of "trust compounds."

**Calculation**  
```
RfT Count = Total Referrals from Customers with Tenure ≥ 24 months

RfT Rate = (RfT Count ÷ Total Customers with Tenure ≥ 24 months) × 100

Example: 25 referrals from 200 long-tenured customers = 12.5% RfT Rate

RfT Share = RfT Count ÷ Total Referrals × 100
Example: 25 ÷ 60 total referrals × 100 = 41.7% of referrals from mature relationships
```

---

### 2.4 Employee Values Retention

**Definition**  
The retention rate of employees in roles that require significant ethical judgment, values interpretation, or human-in-the-loop oversight responsibilities. These are typically customer-facing, compliance, quality assurance, or management positions.

**What It Measures**  
Internal trust and values alignment. Employees who hold judgment-heavy roles are most sensitive to organizational values drift—they see the gap between stated and stress values daily. High turnover in these roles often precedes external trust erosion and indicates systemic misalignment.

**Calculation**  
```
EVR = ((Judgment-Role Employees at Start - Departures) ÷ Judgment-Role Employees at Start) × 100

Example: 20 started year, 3 departed = (20-3) ÷ 20 × 100 = 85% retention

EVR Delta = EVR (Judgment Roles) - Retention Rate (All Employees)
Example: 85% judgment roles - 78% overall = +7% EVR Delta (values-aligned culture)

Negative delta = warning sign of values drift visible to insiders
```

> **SMB Implementation:** If trust is compounding, these numbers improve even without dramatic growth. If distrust is spreading, they erode before revenue does. Track these quarterly and look for divergence from overall business metrics—that divergence is your early warning.

## 3. Alignment Audit Metrics

*Stated Values vs. Stress Values*

These metrics make the gap between your two operating systems—the Analog OS (human values layer) and Digital OS (automated systems layer)—measurable. They answer: "Are our systems expressing our stated values or our stress values?"

### 3.1 Front Page Pass Rate

**Definition**  
The percentage of sampled AI-influenced decisions that pass the "newspaper test"—decisions the organization would confidently defend if they were reported publicly. Based on periodic random sampling of automated outputs.

**What It Measures**  
Systemic integrity under scrutiny. This metric operationalizes the Core Question from your governance framework. It reveals decisions that might be technically compliant but would cause reputational damage if exposed. Identifies the gap between "legal" and "defensible."

**Calculation**  
```
FPPR = (Decisions Passing Front Page Test ÷ Total Sampled Decisions) × 100

Sampling Protocol: Random selection of 10-25 AI-touched decisions monthly
Test Criteria: "Would we confidently explain this to a journalist?"

Example: 22 pass ÷ 25 sampled × 100 = 88% Front Page Pass Rate

Target: 95%+ (the remaining 5% represents acceptable edge cases, not systemic issues)
```

---

### 3.2 Values Drift Score

**Definition**  
A composite score derived from comparing your organization's documented core values against a structured audit of actual system behaviors and outputs. Conducted quarterly to track alignment trajectory over time.

**What It Measures**  
The speed of drift between your Analog OS and Digital OS. Values drift happens gradually—this metric makes the slow accumulation of misalignment visible before it becomes a crisis. Enables course correction while drift is still minor.

**Calculation**  
```
VDS = Σ(Value Alignment Scores) ÷ Number of Core Values

For each documented core value (e.g., Transparency, Fairness, Respect):
- Score 1-5 based on system behavior evidence
- 5 = Fully aligned, 3 = Partially aligned, 1 = Misaligned

Example: Transparency (4) + Fairness (3) + Respect (5) + Privacy (4) = 16
VDS = 16 ÷ 4 values = 4.0 Values Drift Score

VDS Trend = Current VDS - Prior Quarter VDS
Negative trend = drift accelerating; Positive trend = realignment occurring
```

---

### 3.3 Pressure Decision Audit

**Definition**  
A targeted review of decisions made during identified high-pressure periods (quarter-end pushes, crisis response, resource constraints, deadline crunches) compared against decisions made during normal operations.

**What It Measures**  
The gap between stated values and stress values. This metric directly addresses the core thesis: "Your AI is already expressing your values. The question is whether they're your stated values—or your stress values." Reveals what your systems default to under pressure.

**Calculation**  
```
PDA Variance = Normal Period FPPR - High-Pressure Period FPPR

Step 1: Identify high-pressure periods (last week of quarter, incident response, etc.)
Step 2: Sample decisions from those periods
Step 3: Apply Front Page Test to both sets

Example: Normal FPPR = 92%, Pressure FPPR = 78%
PDA Variance = 14 percentage points

High variance = systems perform differently under stress
Target: <5% variance (values hold under pressure)
```

---

### 3.4 Bright Line Incidents

**Definition**  
The count of documented incidents where one of your organization's declared "non-negotiable" values (bright lines) was tested by circumstances, challenged by a stakeholder request, or approached by an automated system's recommendation.

**What It Measures**  
The frequency and nature of boundary tests. As stated in the framework: "Integrity isn't inclusive. It's selective." This metric tracks how often your selectivity is tested and whether your bright lines are actually holding. Zero incidents may indicate either strong culture or blind spots.

**Calculation**  
```
BLI Count = Total Documented Bright Line Incidents per Period

BLI Resolution Rate = (Incidents Where Bright Line Held ÷ Total BLI) × 100

Example: 8 incidents, 7 held firm = 87.5% Resolution Rate

BLI by Value = Count per each declared non-negotiable value
This identifies which values face the most pressure

Track the 1 that didn't hold—that's where systemic vulnerability exists
```

> **SMB Implementation:** Monthly 30-minute review: Sample 10 AI-touched decisions and score them against your SME Integrity Checklist. Document findings. This simple practice creates the data foundation for all four alignment metrics.

## 4. Counterfactual Comparison Metrics

*What Could Have Been*

Since you cannot directly observe the lawsuit you avoided or the crisis that didn't happen, these metrics benchmark your experience against industry baselines and documented incidents at comparable organizations. They make the invisible value of prevention concrete.

### 4.1 Industry Incident Baseline

**Definition**  
A tracked database of publicly reported integrity-related incidents (regulatory actions, lawsuits, settlements, PR crises, data breaches) affecting organizations in your industry segment, updated at least quarterly.

**What It Measures**  
The external risk landscape your integrity investments protect against. This baseline provides the context for understanding what "could have happened" to your organization. It grounds abstract risk in concrete industry precedents and establishes the stakes of misalignment.

**Calculation**  
```
IIB = Count of Industry Incidents per Category per Period

Categories: Regulatory Action | Lawsuit | Settlement | PR Crisis | Data Breach | Other

Industry Incident Rate = Total Industry Incidents ÷ Estimated Companies in Segment

Example: 12 incidents in segment of ~400 companies = 3% annual incident rate

Track severity tiers: Minor (<$50K impact), Moderate ($50K-$500K), Severe (>$500K)
```

---

### 4.2 "We Don't Have That Problem" Index

**Definition**  
A maintained inventory of specific integrity-related problems that are common in your industry but have not occurred in your organization, along with your assessment of why (systems, culture, or luck).

**What It Measures**  
Explicit acknowledgment of risks successfully navigated. This index transforms vague "we've been lucky" sentiment into documented evidence of either systemic protection or unaddressed vulnerability. Updated annually, it creates organizational memory of bullet-dodging.

**Calculation**  
```
WDHTP Index = List of (Issue | Industry Prevalence | Our Status | Attribution)

Attribution Categories:
- S = Systemic (we have controls that prevent this)
- C = Cultural (our values/norms prevent this)
- L = Luck (we haven't faced the trigger conditions)

Protection Confidence = (S + C attributions) ÷ Total Issues × 100

Example: 8 issues, 5 Systemic, 2 Cultural, 1 Luck
Protection Confidence = (5+2) ÷ 8 × 100 = 87.5%

The "L" items are your vulnerability list—these need attention
```

---

### 4.3 Compliance Cost Avoidance

**Definition**  
A calculated estimate of the financial impact your organization has avoided by not experiencing incidents common in your industry, based on average settlement amounts, regulatory fines, and remediation costs from the Industry Incident Baseline.

**What It Measures**  
The dollar value of integrity. This metric translates "risk avoidance" into language finance and leadership understand. While inherently an estimate, it provides a defensible basis for justifying integrity investments and answers: "What is our integrity spending actually buying?"

**Calculation**  
```
CCA = Σ(Industry Avg Cost per Incident Type × Industry Incident Rate × Years Operating)

Step 1: From IIB, get average cost per incident type in your industry
Step 2: Apply incident rate to your operating years
Step 3: Sum across incident types you've avoided

Example:
- Data breach avg = $150K, rate = 5%/year, 8 years = $150K × 0.05 × 8 = $60K
- Lawsuit avg = $200K, rate = 3%/year, 8 years = $200K × 0.03 × 8 = $48K
- Regulatory avg = $75K, rate = 2%/year, 8 years = $75K × 0.02 × 8 = $12K

Total CCA = $120K estimated avoided costs
```

> **SMB Implementation:** Set a quarterly calendar reminder to scan industry news and trade publications for integrity incidents. Maintain a simple log. The research investment is minimal; the strategic value of understanding your risk landscape is substantial.

## 5. Integrity Investment Metrics

*Proving the Spend*

Make the cost of "doing it right" visible so it can be defended and appropriately resourced. These metrics reframe integrity overhead as insurance premium—a deliberate investment with calculable value, not an unfortunate expense.

### 5.1 Human-in-Loop Time Investment

**Definition**  
The total labor hours dedicated to human oversight of AI-influenced decisions, including review of escalated items, quality assurance sampling, veto deliberations, and values alignment checks.

**What It Measures**  
The operational cost of maintaining judgment in your systems. This metric answers skeptics who ask "why can't we just automate this?" by documenting the deliberate human investment that keeps systems aligned. It also tracks whether oversight burden is sustainable.

**Calculation**  
```
HILTI = Σ(Hours Spent on Oversight Activities) per Period

Activities: Escalation Review | QA Sampling | Veto Deliberation | Alignment Checks | Training

HILTI Cost = HILTI × Blended Hourly Rate of Reviewing Personnel

Example: 45 hours/month × $75/hour = $3,375/month oversight investment

HILTI Efficiency = Total Decisions Reviewed ÷ HILTI
Example: 500 decisions ÷ 45 hours = 11.1 decisions/hour review rate
```

---

### 5.2 Values Clarification Sessions

**Definition**  
The aggregate time invested in activities that clarify, document, communicate, or reinforce organizational values—including the Align phase of your strategic framework, team discussions, and values documentation efforts.

**What It Measures**  
Investment in the Analog OS. As stated in the framework: "You cannot code values you haven't clarified." This metric tracks whether you're actually doing the clarification work that makes systemic alignment possible. Low investment here undermines all other integrity metrics.

**Calculation**  
```
VCS = Σ(Hours in Values Clarification Activities) per Quarter

Activities: Align 120 Sessions | Values Workshops | Team Discussions | 
Documentation | Onboarding Values Training | Leadership Alignment

VCS per Employee = VCS ÷ Total Employees

Example: 40 hours quarterly ÷ 15 employees = 2.67 hours/employee/quarter

Benchmark: 2-4 hours per employee per quarter indicates active values maintenance
```

---

### 5.3 Training Investment in Judgment

**Definition**  
The total investment (time and money) in developing employees' capacity for ethical judgment, critical thinking, and values-based decision-making—distinct from technical skills training or compliance checkbox training.

**What It Measures**  
Capacity building for the human layer. AI handles speed; humans handle meaning. This metric tracks whether you're investing in the human capabilities that AI cannot replace—the judgment, context interpretation, and values application that keep systems aligned.

**Calculation**  
```
TIJ = (Training Hours × Attendee Hourly Rate) + Direct Training Costs

Direct Costs: External facilitators | Materials | Programs | Subscriptions
Indirect Costs: Employee time in training (opportunity cost)

TIJ per Employee = TIJ ÷ Total Employees

Example: (120 hours × $50 avg) + $2,000 direct = $8,000 annual
$8,000 ÷ 15 employees = $533/employee/year in judgment development

TIJ Ratio = TIJ ÷ Total Training Budget × 100
What percentage of training investment builds judgment vs. technical skills?
```

---

### 5.4 Checkpoint Processing Cost

**Definition**  
The quantified operational cost of maintaining integrity checkpoints in your automated processes—the time and resources consumed by deliberately designed friction that slows processing to enable human judgment.

**What It Measures**  
The price of deliberate slowness. As stated: "AI removes friction—including moral friction." This metric quantifies what you're paying to add friction back. It's the premium you pay for integrity, analogous to insurance costs, and should be tracked and defended as such.

**Calculation**  
```
CPC = Σ(Checkpoint Time × Processing Volume × Hourly Cost)

Per Checkpoint Type:
- Avg time per review × Monthly volume × Reviewer hourly rate

CPC as % of Operations = CPC ÷ Total Operational Cost × 100

Example: 
- Pre-send review: 3 min × 200/month × $1.25/min = $750
- Escalation handling: 15 min × 45/month × $1.50/min = $1,012
- QA sampling: 5 min × 100/month × $1.25/min = $625

Total CPC = $2,387/month friction cost

Compare to: Compliance Cost Avoidance for ROI framing
```

> **The Reframe:** These aren't overhead costs—they're insurance premiums. Track them as you would any risk mitigation investment. When someone questions the expense, the response is: "This is what integrity costs. Here's what it's protecting us from." (Point to Counterfactual Comparison Metrics.)

## 6. Composite Metric: Integrity Yield

*The Single Score for Stakeholder Visibility*

For board presentations, investor updates, or executive dashboards, a single composite score communicates integrity posture without requiring deep dives into individual metrics. Integrity Yield answers: "Is our integrity strengthening or weakening?"

### The Formula

```
Integrity Yield = 
    (Trust Velocity Score × 0.30) + 
    (Intervention Effectiveness × 0.25) + 
    (Alignment Audit Pass Rate × 0.25) + 
    (Counterfactual Value × 0.20)
```

### Component Definitions

| Component | Description | Weight |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| **Trust Velocity Score** | Composite of RLI Velocity, Forgiveness Rate, and EVR Delta. Normalized to 0-100 scale. | 30% |
| **Intervention Effectiveness** | Composite of Veto Rate, Escalation Rate, and Close Call documentation. Normalized to 0-100 scale. | 25% |
| **Alignment Audit Pass Rate** | Direct use of Front Page Pass Rate, adjusted by Pressure Decision Audit variance. | 25% |
| **Counterfactual Value** | CCA divided by Integrity Investment total, expressed as ROI ratio, then normalized. | 20% |

### Interpretation Guide

| Score | Interpretation |
|-------|----------------|
| **80-100** | Strong integrity posture; systems aligned with stated values |
| **60-79** | Adequate with improvement opportunities; monitor trends |
| **40-59** | Significant gaps; prioritize alignment work |
| **Below 40** | Critical attention needed; risk of systemic misalignment |

## 7. Leading vs. Lagging Indicators

*Your Early Warning System*

By the time you see lagging indicators, the drift has already scaled. Leading indicators are your early warning system—they predict problems before they become crises. Structure your dashboard to prioritize leading indicators.

### Leading Indicators (Predictive)

- Escalation frequency trending up
- Checkpoint triggers increasing
- Employee concerns voiced internally
- Near-miss documentation rising
- Values Drift Score declining
- Pressure Decision Audit variance widening
- Bright Line Incidents increasing
- EVR Delta turning negative
- Front Page Pass Rate dropping
- HILTI efficiency declining

### Lagging Indicators (Reactive)

- Customer complaints filed
- Regulatory inquiry received
- Lawsuit filed or threatened
- Reputation damage in media
- Revenue decline from trust loss
- Key employee departures
- Customer churn spike
- Settlement or fine paid
- Insurance claim filed
- Crisis response activated

> **Dashboard Design Principle:** Structure your integrity dashboard with leading indicators prominent and lagging indicators as reference context. The goal is to act on leading signals before lagging consequences manifest. When a leading indicator triggers, it should prompt investigation—not wait for confirmation from lagging indicators.

The Challenge

Whole Person, Fragmented Care

Dr. Maya Chen runs Thrive Integrative Health — a 12-person clinic with 4 therapists, 3 nutritionists, 2 health coaches, 1 nurse practitioner, and 2 admin staff. They treat the whole person: a patient dealing with anxiety might see a therapist weekly, a nutritionist monthly, and a health coach bi-weekly.

It's great care. It's also an operational nightmare.

The coordination problem: When a patient sees 3 providers, each one operates in their own silo. The therapist doesn't know the nutritionist recommended eliminating caffeine. The health coach doesn't know the therapist is working on sleep hygiene. Each provider spends 15-20 minutes per shared patient just trying to stay in sync.

The engagement gap: Between appointments, patients are on their own. Three providers give three separate sets of instructions. Patients forget, mix things up, or feel overwhelmed. No one coordinates the between-visit experience.

The admin burden: Dr. Chen spends 30% of her time on non-clinical work. Treatment summaries, wellness program reports, care plan documentation, and making sure nothing falls through the cracks across 420 patients and 9 providers.

The Question

How do you make 9 providers feel like one unified care team — to each other and to the patient — without tripling the admin staff?

Phase 1

Strategic Decision (Strategy 120)

Step 1: Strategic Assessment with Alfred

/exec-strategic-advisor — "I run a 12-person integrative health clinic with 420 patients. Our providers work in silos. I want to use AI to unify the care experience and reduce provider admin time. Is this viable in healthcare's regulatory environment?"

Alfred produces:

  • Market analysis of AI in integrative health (Welkin, Healthie, SimplePractice)
  • Regulatory landscape: HIPAA constraints on AI processing patient data
  • Three scaling models with trade-offs:

Model A: AI coordinates provider-to-provider only (internal sync) → solves silos, doesn't help engagement

Model B: AI coordinates providers AND delivers between-visit patient touchpoints → solves both problems

Model C: AI adds predictive care (flags at-risk patients, suggests adjustments) → maximum impact, maximum regulatory complexity

Key Insight

The biggest win isn't fancy AI — it's unified care summaries. A patient seeing three providers currently gets three separate sets of instructions. AI can synthesize those into one coherent message: "Here's what your whole team wants you to focus on this week." That single capability transforms the patient experience from fragmented to integrated. Model B is the right fit.

Critical tension flagged by Higgins: Legal needs maximum data minimization. Care coordination needs data sharing. Resolution: AI processes care plan summaries and stated goals only — never raw session transcripts or clinical notes. Providers review and approve all patient-facing content. The AI sees what a care coordinator would see, not what a clinician documents privately.

Decision Record (DEC-2026-011)

Values Alignment Check:

  • Human-centered: Providers own all clinical decisions. AI coordinates, never recommends treatment.

Kai's care coordination workflow (the key deliverable):

AFTER EACH VISIT (AI-driven, provider reviews)
───────────────────────────────────────────────
1. Provider enters care summary (goals, recommendations, homework)
2. AI compares against other providers' active care plans
3. AI flags conflicts or synergies:
   CONFLICT: "Therapist assigned journaling before bed. Health
   coach assigned evening workouts. These compete for same time."
   SYNERGY: "Nutritionist's anti-inflammatory diet aligns with
   therapist's stress reduction work. Reinforce connection."
4. Flagged items appear in other providers' dashboards within 1 hr
5. Providers acknowledge or adjust (2-min task vs. 15-min search)

UNIFIED PATIENT TOUCHPOINT (AI-drafted, provider-approved)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
6. AI synthesizes all active care plans into one weekly message:
   "Here's what your care team wants you to focus on this week:
    - From your therapist: Practice 5-4-3-2-1 grounding exercise
    - From your nutritionist: Try 2 anti-inflammatory recipes
    - From your coach: 3 walks this week, 20 min each"
7. Primary provider reviews and approves (3 min)
8. Patient receives unified message

MID-WEEK CHECK-IN (AI-driven, provider monitors)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────
9.  AI sends: "How are things going? Which goals felt manageable?"
10. Patient responds (free text or quick rating)
11. AI routes: positive → logged | struggling → flagged | concerning → alert

CARE TEAM SYNC (AI-generated, weekly)
─────────────────────────────────────
12. Weekly digest per multi-provider patient:
    what each provider is working on, engagement, flags
13. Providers review in 5 min (replaces 15-min chart review)

MONTHLY REPORTING
─────────────────
14. Patient: progress report across all providers
15. Employer: anonymized aggregate wellness report
16. Insurance: treatment summary with goals and outcomes

Dallas's safety policy (healthcare-specific — non-negotiable):

RED Self-harm, suicidal ideation, abuse, acute crisis Alert primary provider AND therapist immediately. No AI response. < 5 min
ORANGE Medication side effects, severe symptom changes, emergency request Alert relevant provider. AI: "I'm letting your care team know." < 1 hour
YELLOW Missed 2+ check-ins, no progress 3+ weeks, frustration Flag for provider review. Suggest proactive outreach. < 24 hrs
GREEN Positive responses, goal updates, scheduling questions AI handles autonomously. Logged for session prep. Standard
NEVER Clinical advice, diagnosis, medication, therapeutic interventions "That's a great question for your [provider] at your next visit." Hard block

Summary

Key Takeaways

1

A 12-person clinic gets enterprise health system coordination. Multi-provider care coordination typically requires a dedicated care coordinator ($60K+/yr) or enterprise platform ($50K+/yr). Thrive gets both through 30 specialized agents at a fraction of the cost.

2

The "shared brain" transforms patient experience. Patients stop managing three separate healthcare relationships. One unified weekly message. One set of priorities. One care team that actually knows what the others are doing. 72% between-visit engagement proves patients want this.

3

Coordination overhead drops 80%. 15 minutes to 3 minutes per shared patient. That's 114 hours/month of reclaimed clinical time — roughly 90 additional patient visits of revenue that was being burned on internal logistics.

4

Employer wellness opens a B2B growth channel. $3.4M → $4.2M by adding corporate accounts. HR Directors get dashboards, employees get coordinated care, and the practice gets recurring contract revenue alongside patient memberships.

5

HIPAA compliance is designed in, not bolted on. AI processes care plan summaries only — never raw clinical notes, diagnoses, or medication lists. Patient consent is granular and revocable. The data architecture was the first deliverable, not an afterthought.

6

The safety policy is non-negotiable. Five tiers from RED (immediate crisis alert) to NEVER (hard-blocked clinical advice). "That's a great question for your provider at your next visit" is a guardrail, not a suggestion. AI coordinates care — it never delivers it.
